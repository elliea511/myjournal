# Numbers Are Our Communication With the Stars

Today I went to see my friend Caroline at her clinic for her birthday, and we briefly started talking about the way we appreciate movies.

Her friend showed me a picture of a Wicked-themed cake she had made for Caroline’s birthday last year, and I immediately got excited because *Wicked* was such an incredible movie to me. I was so happy to realize that Caroline viewed it in a similar way.

I asked her why she loved it so much, and she told me she had seen it on Broadway during a very complicated divorce. It completely blew her away. She practically sobbed while holding hands with her best friend because she almost immediately recognized it as a testament to friendship.

I had gone into *Wicked* for completely different reasons. Ariana Grande, my favorite artist, was playing Glinda, my favorite character from *The Wizard of Oz*, and I became obsessed almost immediately.

What I loved about our conversation was realizing that the same story had reached us from two completely different directions.

Movies are so easy to dismiss as just entertainment, but there is so much to learn from them. I think some of the most introspective people are the ones who can see what is actually being said underneath the obvious story. There is so much nuance and depth in almost any movie if you are willing to look for it.

*Wicked* can be enjoyed because it is beautiful and lively and interesting, but if you start reading between the lines, the story becomes much bigger. It touches on things that eventually come up in almost everyone’s life: friendship, loyalty, power, judgment, identity, misunderstanding, and the uncomfortable fact that people are rarely entirely good or entirely bad. We are all collections of contradictions.

It was just a really fun conversation for me because I do not get to talk about those kinds of complexities very often.

When I got home, I started telling Isaiah about it. Then I saw something on my phone about numerology.

A couple of days earlier, we had watched a Steven Spielberg movie about contact with aliens, and the male lead communicated through an affinity for math. That came back to me when I started thinking about why numbers are suddenly so interesting to me.

I think part of the reason they interest me now is because I recently realized that I have almost the opposite relationship with numbers in my own head. Visually, they are boring to me. They do not seem to carry meaning as naturally as images, words, sounds, or scenes do. And yet numbers might be one of the most meaningful things humans have ever created or discovered.

Numbers are everywhere.

We have barely left this planet, and math is what allowed us to do it. Our understanding of the universe is built through measurement, pattern, distance, time, mass, force, probability, and equations. Physics is how we try to describe reality, and so much of physics is expressed through numbers.

Numbers are our communication with the stars.

Isn’t that beautiful?

Then the numerology chart made me think about astrology because it looked so much like a zodiac chart. That made me think about how quickly people dismiss astrology as nonsense.

I understand that astrology is not accepted as established science, but I still find the instinct to dismiss the entire idea interesting. We are so rooted in our current understanding of reality that sometimes we act as if anything outside it must automatically be ridiculous.

Why would it be strange to wonder whether the movements and positions of celestial bodies could have some relationship to us? The moon physically affects the Earth. People casually joke about full moons affecting human behavior. We use the motion of planets and stars to understand time, seasons, navigation, and the structure of the universe. So I do not think it is absurd to at least wonder whether there are relationships we do not fully understand yet.

People will study numbers to understand the universe, but the idea of using patterns to understand ourselves is suddenly treated as silly.

And somehow astrology is often specifically framed as a dumb woman thing.

That is the part I keep coming back to.

Women are often taught to be introspective. We are expected to understand emotion, notice behavior, read people, accept criticism, examine ourselves, and make sense of complicated relationships. Then when women become interested in a system that tries to connect personality, behavior, timing, fate, and the universe, that curiosity is mocked as irrational.

Meanwhile, entire religious and philosophical systems have existed for thousands of years trying to answer the exact same questions: Who are we? Why are we here? Is there order to any of this? Is our path predetermined? What forces shape us? What happens next?

We accept some systems because they are old, powerful, institutional, or traditionally male-led, while other systems are treated as unserious because of the people most associated with them.

I am not saying I have proven astrology because obviously I have not. I do not have a degree in physics, astronomy, or anything remotely related to this. This is just where my brain went.

A birthday visit turned into a conversation about *Wicked*. That turned into thinking about how stories hold meaning. Then I went home, saw a numerology chart, remembered a movie about communicating with aliens through math, and ended up thinking about numbers, stars, astrology, religion, women, and who gets to decide which ways of making sense of the universe are respectable.

What an odd and dysfunctional world we live in.

## What I actually mean by astrology

I do not think astrology is a science, and I do not think a birth chart determines who someone is or what will happen to them. That is not really why I find it interesting.

To me, astrology is a form of spirituality. It is a system that asks you to look inward and outward at the same time. You look at yourself, your relationships, your patterns, your reactions, and your place within something much larger than you. It asks, **how am I a part of this?**

I think there is value in any system that encourages that question.

We spend so much of our lives treating the world as something happening outside of us. Other people are the problem. Society is the problem. Our circumstances are the problem. The universe is this enormous thing somewhere above us that we are observing from a distance. But we are not outside of any of it. We are participants. We affect other people and they affect us. Our environment shapes us and we shape it in return. We are physical objects existing inside a constantly changing and expanding universe.

In that sense, I love the idea that we are the universe experiencing itself over and over again.

That does not mean every pattern we notice is real. Introspection can become overinterpretation. But a lack of awareness can become a lack of interpretation entirely, and I am not convinced that is somehow more intelligent.

That is part of why the way people mock astrology bothers me, especially when that mockery is directed at women. Astrology has become heavily associated with women, and suddenly a system based around examining yourself, your relationships, patterns, ambiguity, and your place in the universe is treated like evidence that you are stupid.

I find that strange.

Women spend so much of their lives being asked, directly and indirectly, to examine themselves. How do I look? How did I sound? Was I too much? Was I not enough? How did that person feel? What did I contribute to that interaction? What could I have done differently? We are constantly taught to interpret ourselves and the people around us.

Of course that hyperawareness can become unhealthy. But the opposite can be unhealthy too. Never asking what role you play in anything does not make you more rational. Sometimes it just makes you less aware.

So no, I do not need astrology to be objectively correct for me to think there is something intellectually valuable about it. I do not need the planets to dictate my personality. I do not need a chart to predict my future.

I just think there is something deeply human about looking at the universe and asking where you fit inside it.

And I think **“How am I a part of this?”** is a more useful question than living your entire life believing everything exists completely outside of you.
