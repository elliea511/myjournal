# Why Do I Have All These Random Body Variations?

This rabbit hole started with a tendon.

If I touch my thumb and pinkie together and flex my wrist, the palmaris longus tendon shows up on one side and not the other. Mine is absent on the right. That sent me down a whole chain of questions about the weird little anatomical differences people can have without ever realizing they are unusual.

Then I started listing the other things I have noticed about myself.

- My ring finger is longer than my index finger.
- I have unattached earlobes.
- I have Darwin’s tubercle on both ears.
- I can do several of the little inherited-trait/body-variation tests we talked through.
- I had an extra baby tooth and then an extra adult tooth in the same place on the upper left side, between my incisor and canine. It was slightly smaller than my normal incisor but otherwise looked like a regular tooth.

The tooth is probably the strangest one to me because it happened twice: once in my baby teeth and again when the adult teeth came in.

What I like about this whole topic is that none of these things feel unusual when they are just part of your own body. You can go years assuming everybody is built basically the same way, then notice one tendon is missing and suddenly realize human anatomy is full of small variations.

And apparently that is enough to send me from looking at my wrist to inspecting my ears, fingers, and dental history.

## Things this rabbit hole made me want to know

- Why do some people have the palmaris longus and others do not?
- Why can it be present on only one side?
- What was the tendon useful for in the first place?
- Does having or not having it change hand strength?
- What exactly is Darwin’s tubercle?
- How common is it to grow an extra tooth in the same location in both the baby and adult dentition?

This is exactly the kind of question I want to keep here: something tiny I noticed that turned into an hour of wondering why humans are built the way we are.
